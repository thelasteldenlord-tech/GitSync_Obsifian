# Follow-up & Rejection Messages

> Professional templates for maintaining relationships after rejection

**Last Updated**: 2025-10-28

---

## Guiding Principles

When a prospect says "no":
1. **Thank them sincerely** - They gave us their time
2. **Stay professional** - Every interaction builds reputation
3. **Leave door open** - Today's "no" may be tomorrow's "yes"
4. **No sales pitches** - Respect their decision
5. **Make it memorable** - Stand out from others who disappear

---

## Template 1: Standard Rejection Follow-up

### Email/Letter Template

**Subject**: Thank you for your consideration

Dear [Contact Name],

I wanted to take a moment to personally thank you for the time you and your team invested in considering [Your Company] for your [service type] needs.

While I understand we weren't the right fit for your needs at this time, I genuinely appreciated the opportunity to learn about [Their Company] and the professional way you handled our discussions.

We are a young, growing company that learns from every interaction. If you have a moment and are willing, I'd welcome any feedback about what we could have done better or differently. Your insights would help us improve.

Should your needs change in the future, or if there's ever a service we can provide that better fits your requirements, please don't hesitate to reach out. We'd welcome the opportunity to work with you.

Thank you again for your time and consideration. I wish you and [Their Company] continued success.

Sincerely,

[Your Name]
[Your Company]
[Contact Information]

## Template 2: Request for Feedback

Use when you want to learn from the experience:

**Subject**: Quick question - Your feedback?

Hi [Contact Name],

Thank you again for considering us for your [service type] needs. While we weren't selected this time, I'm always looking to improve.

Would you mind sharing briefly what led to your decision? Specifically:
- Was it pricing, timing, service offering, or something else?
- What could we have done better in our proposal or presentation?
- Is there anything we could offer in the future that would be valuable?

I understand you're busy, so even a sentence or two would be incredibly helpful. Our goal is to become the best service provider in Southwest Virginia, and your honest feedback helps us get there.

Thanks again, and best wishes with your new service provider.

[Your Name]

## Template 3: Long-term Follow-up

Use for "maybe later" situations - send 3-6 months after initial rejection:

**Subject**: Checking in - [Your Company]

Hi [Contact Name],

I hope this message finds you well. We spoke [timeframe] about [service type] for [Their Company], and while the timing wasn't right then, I wanted to reach out and see how things are going.

[Optional: specific detail you remember from your conversation]

If you're still happy with your current arrangement, that's great - I'm glad you found a good fit. But if circumstances have changed or you'd like to revisit our discussion, I'd love to hear from you.

No pressure at all - just wanted to stay in touch and let you know we're here if you need us.

Best regards,

[Your Name]

## Template 4: Thank You Card (Handwritten)

**For**: High-value prospects, particularly professional interactions

**Send to**:
- Main decision maker
- Anyone who spent significant time with you
- Receptionist who was helpful

**Message**:

```
[Date]

Dear [Name],

Thank you for taking the time to meet with me and consider
[Your Company] for your facility needs.

I appreciated learning about [Their Company] and enjoyed
our conversation about [specific topic you discussed].

While we weren't selected this time, I hope we can stay
in touch. If anything changes or we can help in the future,
please don't hesitate to reach out.

Wishing you continued success,

[Your Signature]
[Your Name]
```

**Why handwritten?**
- Shows extra effort and sincerity
- Memorable in digital age
- Professional and personal
- Keeps you top-of-mind

## What NOT to Do

### Never:
❌ Make another sales pitch in follow-up
❌ Argue about their decision
❌ Badmouth whoever they chose
❌ Show frustration or disappointment
❌ Apply pressure for reconsideration
❌ Contact repeatedly after "no"
❌ Ask for referrals (in rejection message)

### Always:
✅ Thank them genuinely
✅ Respect their decision
✅ Keep it brief
✅ Maintain professionalism
✅ Focus on the relationship, not the sale
✅ Proofread carefully
✅ Send promptly (within 24-48 hours)

## Timeline for Follow-up

### Immediate (Within 24-48 hours)
- Thank you email/letter
- Internal documentation update

### Short-term (1 week later)
- Handwritten thank you card arrives
- Mark file appropriately in system

### Medium-term (3-6 months)
- Follow-up check-in (if appropriate)
- Only if door was left open

### Long-term (12+ months)
- Periodic check-in (if relationship warranted)
- Holiday cards to maintain connection

## Special Situations

### They Chose a Competitor

**Don't**:
- Ask "what did they offer?"
- Badmouth the competitor
- Try to undercut after the fact

**Do**:
- "I hope they serve you well"
- "If anything changes, we're here"
- Move on graciously

### They Decided Not to Use Any Service

**Response**:
"I understand. If you decide to explore services in the future, I'd love to discuss it. Best wishes."

### Budget Constraints

**Response**:
"I completely understand budget considerations. If your budget changes or you'd like to explore options, please reach out."

### Timing Wasn't Right

**Response**:
"Timing is important. May I check back with you in [timeframe] to see if circumstances have changed?"

## Internal Documentation

After sending follow-up, update the client profile:

### Mark Status Appropriately
- **DNC (Do Not Contact)**: Hard no, asked not to contact
- **Follow-up [Date]**: Open to future contact
- **Monitor**: Keep an eye on, very long-term
- **Closed-Lost**: No longer pursuing

### Document Details
- Date of follow-up sent
- Type of follow-up (email, card, etc.)
- Any response received
- Next action date (if applicable)

### Learn from Experience
In the prospect file, note:
- Why we lost
- What we could improve
- Lessons learned
- Pricing feedback

## Measuring Success

### Good Follow-up Results In:
- Positive responses from prospects
- Referrals (sometimes)
- Future opportunities
- Enhanced reputation
- Learning and improvement

### Track:
- Response rate to follow-ups
- Future business from past rejections
- Referrals from prospects who said no
- Reputation improvements

## Examples of Actual Messages

### Example 1: Manufacturing Facility

"Dear John,

Thank you for the time you spent with me last Thursday showing me around your facility. I enjoyed learning about your manufacturing process - the precision work you do is impressive.

While I understand you've decided to continue with your current cleaning service, I appreciated the professional way you evaluated our proposal.

If circumstances ever change, we'd welcome the opportunity to work with you. Best wishes for continued success.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]"

### Example 2: After Pricing Concern

"Hi Sarah,

Thanks again for considering us. I understand our pricing didn't fit your current budget, and I respect that decision.

We're always looking to provide better value, so if you're willing to share what price point would have worked for you, I'd appreciate the feedback. It helps us understand market expectations.

Either way, thanks for your time, and I wish you the best.

[Your Name]"
