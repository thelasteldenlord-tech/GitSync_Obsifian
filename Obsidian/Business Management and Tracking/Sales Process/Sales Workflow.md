# Sales Workflow

> Complete guide to our sales process from lead to client

**Last Updated**: 2025-10-28

---

## Overview

Our sales process is designed to be professional, ethical, and relationship-focused. We're building long-term client relationships, not just closing deals.

### Sales Philosophy

1. **Quality over quantity** - Target right-fit clients
2. **Education-first** - Help prospects understand their needs
3. **No pressure** - Clients should want our services
4. **Long-term thinking** - Every interaction builds reputation
5. **Gracious in rejection** - Today's "no" might be tomorrow's "yes"

---

```

    ↓         ↓         ↓          ↓          ↓          ↓
  Research   Follow    Schedule   Present    Address    Contract
  Profile    Response  Meeting    Proposal   Concerns   Sign
```

## Stage 1: Prospecting

**Objective**: Identify potential clients who fit our ideal customer profile

### Lead Sources
- Geographic research (Southwest VA businesses)
- Industry targeting (manufacturing, industrial)
- Referrals and networking
- Online research
- Local business directories

### Ideal Customer Profile
- Location: Within 50-mile service radius
- Size: Adequate revenue potential
- Type: Industrial/commercial properties
- Need: Cleaning and/or security services
- Professionalism: BBB-rated or equivalent

### Activities
1. **Research potential clients**
   - Use [[Lead Management|Lead Management]] system
   - Document in [[Customer Profile|customer profile]]
   - Prioritize based on fit

2. **Initial qualification**
   - Check location (within service area?)
   - Estimate property size
   - Assess service needs
   - Check online reputation

3. **Create prospect file**
   - Use [[Customer Profile]] template
   - Store in `Leads/Prospects/`
   - Set research tasks
   - Assign priority level

**Tools**: Google Maps, BBB, industry directories, LinkedIn

## Stage 2: Initial Contact

**Objective**: Make professional first impression and gauge interest

### Preparation
Before contacting:
- [ ] Research company thoroughly
- [ ] Identify decision maker
- [ ] Prepare value proposition
- [ ] Review current availability
- [ ] Have calendar ready

### Contact Methods (in order of preference)
1. **Warm introduction** (referral)
2. **Phone call** to front desk/reception
3. **In-person visit** (if appropriate for industry)
4. **Email** (for larger organizations)
5. **LinkedIn** (for professional services)

### Initial Contact Script

See [[Sales Process/Initial Contact Script]] for detailed scripts.

**Phone approach**:
```
"Good morning, my name is [Name] with [Company]. We provide
professional cleaning and security services for manufacturing
facilities in the Southwest Virginia area. I'd like to speak
with whoever handles your facility management. Would that be
you, or could you direct me to the right person?"
```

**Goals for first contact**:
- Introduce company professionally
- Confirm decision maker
- Gauge initial interest
- Request meeting or follow-up
- Leave good impression

### Response Tracking

**Document everything**:
- Date and time of contact
- Person contacted
- Response received
- Next steps agreed
- Follow-up date

**Update prospect status**:
- Not interested → [[Sales Process/Rejection Follow-up]]
- Interested → Schedule meeting
- Maybe later → Set follow-up reminder
- Wrong contact → Research correct person

## Stage 3: Qualification & Discovery

**Objective**: Determine if we're a good mutual fit

### Qualifying Questions

**Business Understanding**:
- What's your current cleaning/security arrangement?
- What challenges are you experiencing?
- What's most important to you in a service provider?
- When does your current contract expire?

**Needs Assessment**:
- Property size and layout?
- Operating hours?
- Special requirements (industrial, chemical, security)?
- Frequency of service needed?

**Decision Process**:
- Who's involved in the decision?
- What's your timeline?
- What's your budget range?
- What would make you switch providers?

**Qualification Criteria**:
- [ ] Within our service area
- [ ] Property size matches capability
- [ ] Budget aligns with services
- [ ] Decision timeline reasonable
- [ ] Mutual fit apparent

### Disqualification Signals
- Too far from service area
- Unrealistic budget expectations
- Unreasonable demands
- Poor communication/unprofessional
- Red flags (payment issues, reputation)

**If disqualified**: Exit graciously, leave door open for future.

## Stage 4: Proposal Development

**Objective**: Create compelling, accurate service proposal

### Site Survey
**Always conduct site visit before proposing**:
- [ ] Walk entire property
- [ ] Measure key areas
- [ ] Note special requirements
- [ ] Photograph (with permission)
- [ ] Meet key personnel
- [ ] Ask additional questions

**Template**: [[Templates/Site Survey]]

### Proposal Components

1. **Executive Summary**
   - Client's needs/challenges
   - Our proposed solution
   - Key benefits
   - Investment overview

2. **Scope of Work**
   - Detailed service description
   - Frequency and schedule
   - Areas covered
   - Equipment and supplies
   - Quality standards

3. **Pricing**
   - Clear, itemized pricing
   - Payment terms
   - Contract length
   - Renewal terms

4. **Company Information**
   - Experience and qualifications
   - Insurance and bonding
   - References (if applicable)
   - Why choose us

5. **Next Steps**
   - Decision timeline
   - Implementation plan
   - Contact information

**Template**: [[Templates/Client Proposal]]

### Pricing Guidelines
- Profitable for us
- Competitive for market
- Transparent (no hidden fees)
- Justified by value

## Stage 5: Presentation & Negotiation

**Objective**: Present proposal and address concerns

### Proposal Presentation

**In-person preferred**:
1. Review their needs (confirm understanding)
2. Present solution (walk through proposal)
3. Highlight value (focus on benefits)
4. Invite questions (listen actively)
5. Address concerns (be honest)
6. Discuss next steps

**Key Skills**:
- Listen more than talk
- Focus on value, not price
- Be confident but not pushy
- Admit when you don't know something
- Never badmouth competitors

### Common Objections

**"Too expensive"**:
- Understand their budget
- Explain value justification
- Offer scaled options if possible
- Compare total cost of ownership

**"Happy with current provider"**:
- Respect that
- Ask what they like about them
- Offer to keep info for future
- Leave professional impression

**"Need to think about it"**:
- Understandable response
- Ask about specific concerns
- Offer to provide more info
- Set specific follow-up date

**"Have to talk to partner/boss"**:
- Expected in business
- Offer to meet with them too
- Provide materials to share
- Set follow-up date

### Negotiation Principles
- Know your bottom line (before meeting)
- Be flexible where possible
- Stand firm on quality
- Create win-win outcomes
- Don't devalue your services

## Stage 6: Closing

**Objective**: Finalize agreement and secure commitment

### Closing Signals

**Client is ready when they**:
- Ask about start dates
- Discuss implementation details
- Introduce you to other staff
- Ask about contract terms
- Show enthusiasm

### Closing Techniques

**Direct close**:
"Based on everything we've discussed, shall we move forward?"

**Alternative close**:
"Would you prefer to start on the 1st or the 15th?"

**Summary close**:
"Let me summarize what we've agreed to... Does that sound right?"

**Trial close** (during discussion):
"If we could address [concern], would you be ready to move forward?"

### Contract Execution

1. **Review contract together**
   - Walk through terms
   - Answer questions
   - Clarify expectations
   - Ensure understanding

2. **Obtain signatures**
   - Both parties sign
   - Date clearly
   - Provide client copy immediately
   - File our copy securely

3. **Collect initial information**
   - Emergency contacts
   - Access authorizations
   - Keys/codes (if applicable)
   - Special instructions

4. **Celebrate (internally)**
   - Thank the client
   - Remain professional
   - Don't oversell after close

**Document**: [[Client Access Policy]] requirements

## Stage 7: Onboarding

**Objective**: Ensure smooth transition and exceed expectations

### Implementation Checklist

- [ ] Enter client in system
- [ ] Create client file structure
- [ ] Schedule start date
- [ ] Assign service team
- [ ] Conduct site orientation
- [ ] Provide client orientation
- [ ] Establish communication protocol
- [ ] Set first review date

### First 30 Days
- **Week 1**: Extra attention, confirm satisfaction
- **Week 2**: Check in, address any issues
- **Week 3**: Ensure routine established
- **Week 4**: First formal review meeting

### Set Expectations
- How to reach us
- Response times
- Quality standards
- Feedback process
- Issue resolution

**Goal**: Exceed expectations early, build trust quickly

## Handling Rejection

**Objective**: Maintain professionalism and leave door open

See [[Sales Process/Rejection Follow-up]] for detailed procedures.

### When Prospect Says No

1. **Thank them sincerely**
   - For their time
   - For consideration
   - For professional interaction

2. **Ask for feedback** (optional)
   - Why did they choose differently?
   - What could we have done better?
   - Was there something missing?

3. **Leave door open**
   - "If circumstances change..."
   - "We'd love to work with you in future"
   - "May I follow up in 6 months?"

4. **Follow up professionally**
   - Send [[last_impressions|thank you note]]
   - Mark file "DNC" if hard no
   - Set reminder if "maybe later"

**Never**:
- Argue or pressure
- Badmouth competitor they chose
- Burn bridges
- Show disappointment/frustration
