# Drakengard Hub

**Release Year:** 2003 (JP), 2004 (NA)
**Platform:** PlayStation 2
**Developer:** Cavia
**Director:** Yoko Taro

> [!summary] Overview
> Drakengard (Drag-On Dragoon in Japan) is the first game released in the series. Set in a medieval fantasy world torn by war, it follows Caim, a prince who makes a pact with a dragon named Angelus to save his life. The game is famous for its dark themes, multiple endings, and Ending E which bridges to the NieR series.

## Story & Lore

### Main Story
The world is protected by four magical seals. When they begin to fail, the Empire rises, worshipping beings called the Watchers. Caim, mortally wounded in battle, makes a pact with the dragon Angelus to survive and protect his sister Furiae, the Goddess who maintains the final seal.

### The Pact System
Central to the game's lore: humans make pacts with creatures to gain power, but each pact requires a sacrifice:
- **Caim** → Lost his voice
- **Leonard** → Lost his sight (in some interpretations)
- **Arioch** → Lost her womb/ability to bear children
- **Inuart** → Lost his hair (later makes a darker pact)

### Multiple Endings
Drakengard features 5 endings (A-E), each branching from different points:
- **Ending A:** The "normal" ending
- **Ending B:** Reveals more about Furiae
- **Ending C:** Dark turn for certain characters
- **Ending D:** Alternative conclusion
- **Ending E:** The apocalyptic ending that creates the NieR timeline

### Key Themes
- The cost of power (pacts require sacrifice)
- Love and obsession
- The cycle of violence
- Moral ambiguity - no true heroes or villains

## Universe Context

### Timeline Position
**Chronologically Second** (but first released) - Takes place after Drakengard 3's events

### Connection to Other Games
- **← Drakengard 3:** Prequel that explains the world's magical nature
- **→ Drakengard 2:** Alternative timeline from Ending A
- **→ NieR Replicant/Gestalt:** Born from the catastrophic Ending E

### The Bridge to NieR
**Ending E** is crucial: Caim and Angelus are transported to modern-day Tokyo (our world) in the year 2003. They're shot down by the Japanese military, but Angelus's body spawns White Chlorination Syndrome, a magical disease. This apocalypse eventually leads to the events of NieR thousands of years later.
