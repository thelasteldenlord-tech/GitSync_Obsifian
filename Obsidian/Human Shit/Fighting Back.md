## Immediate Steps to Stop the Scammer and Protect Your Mother

### 1. **Secure Financial Access**
- **Contact her bank immediately** to freeze accounts and stop further transactions.  Request a new account if necessary.
- Ask the bank to flag suspicious activity and set up transaction alerts.
- If she has given the scammer access to her accounts, change all passwords and revoke access.

### 2. **Report the Scam**
File reports with:
- **Federal Trade Commission (FTC)**: [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov)
- **FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)**: [ic3.gov](https://ic3.gov)
- **National Elder Fraud Hotline**: 1-833-FRAUD-11 (1-833-372-8311)
- **Adult Protective Services** in your state 

Include all details: scammer’s name, contact info, payment methods, and transaction records.

### 3. **Monitor and Block the Scammer**
- If the scammer is using social media or messaging apps, **log into your mother’s account (with her consent or through legal means)** and block the scammer. 
- Adjust privacy settings to limit who can contact her.
- Consider using monitoring tools like Carefull or similar services to track financial activity.

### 4. **Seek Legal Protection**
- If your mother is unable to make sound financial decisions, consider **filing for conservatorship or guardianship** to legally manage her affairs.
- Set up a **durable power of attorney** if not already in place.

### 5. **Emotional Support and Recovery**
- Approach her with empathy, not blame. Victims often feel shame and may resist help.
- Encourage counseling or support groups for scam victims.
- Educate her on romance scam red flags without judgment.

### 6. **Recover Funds (Limited Success)**
- While recovery is difficult, especially with wire transfers or gift cards, **act quickly**.  Banks may reverse recent transactions.
- Do **not** pay for “recovery services” — these are often scams themselves.
